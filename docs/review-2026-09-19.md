# Build review, 19 September 2026

Written against `docs/spec.md` version 2.1 of 18 September 2026. The
repository is shared separately, so nothing here quotes code.

Repository state at the time of writing: branch `develop`, 48 merged pull
requests, 63 recorded decisions, 12 app DocTypes, 17 services, 655 tests.

## How to read this

One section per phase, in the spec's own order. Each says what the spec
asked for, what was built, and then my own review of it. The reviews are
the point. They say where I think the work is thin, where I would look
hardest, and where I changed my mind, because a summary that only reports
success is not worth reviewing.

Everything I want from you is at the end, in one place.

## P00 Baseline

**Asked for:** inspect the repository and runtime, pin the source, rules and
runtime candidates, verify the official examples, record scope and
compatibility lanes.

**Built:** PINT AE Billing 1.0.4, UBL 2.1 XSD, both Schematron layers and
all official examples pinned with checksums in `docs/standards-lock.json`.
SaxonC chosen as the XSLT runtime, because the compiled rules are XSLT 2.0
and the usual engine only speaks 1.0. Evidence recorded under
`docs/evidence/p00`.

**Review.** This is the part I trust most, because every value is cited to
a file and a line in the publication rather than to memory. Two things a
reviewer should know. One published credit note example fails its own
schema, recorded as D008, and is excluded as a positive case rather than
worked around. And the self-billing family was identified here as a
separate package we did not pin, which is why 389 and 261 are still not
possible; that was the right call at the time and is now the thing holding
self-billing up.

## P01 Contracts and domain

**Asked for:** canonical and finding schemas, deterministic hashing,
scope and tax decision tables, money examples, a reference serializer and a
locked validator wrapper. Pure tests, independent of Frappe and providers.

**Built:** the canonical invoice as data rather than code, deterministic
JSON encoding with two separate hashes, the finding and result shapes with
one readiness rule, the money rules, the company mode and document type
matrices, the adapter contract, and the serializer.

**Review.** The separation held up. Everything here still runs without
Frappe and without a provider, which is what let three independent reviews
read it in isolation later.

Two weaknesses worth a reviewer's attention. The canonical model grew under
pressure rather than by design: `tax_amount` on a line, `tax_inclusive_aed`
on the totals and `body` on an artifact reference were all added in P09
because something downstream could not be done without them. Each addition
is recorded, but a model that needs four additions at the end was not quite
right at the start.

And the money rules check rather than calculate, which is correct, but that
invariant was broken twice without anyone noticing: the serializer worked
out line VAT from the rate, and the group base was summed rather than read.
Both were found in P09 by a reviewer, not by the rules themselves. An
invariant that nothing enforces is a comment.

## P02 Configuration and masters

**Asked for:** app, module, roles, settings and provider configuration with
encryption proof, seller and party mapping, tax and unit mappings,
idempotent fixtures.

**Built:** the module, two roles, settings, the provider connection with
encrypted credential rows, seller and party profiles, and the tax category
mapping. A clean install starts every company switched off.

**Review.** The rule I am most confident in is that a claim and evidence
never touch: what somebody tells us about a party is one field and what a
lookup found is another, and no manual path can set the second. That is
tested from two directions and a crafted payload was checked against it in
P09.

The weakness is that this is the phase with the most user interface and the
least human use. The nineteen hand test cases written for it were never
run, and four phases of work have landed on top since. I would not put this
in front of a client until somebody has clicked through it.

## P03 ERP extraction

**Asked for:** a pinned source map, selected address resolution, line, tax,
discount and currency extraction, and an ordinary invoice and credit note
through to XML.

**Built:** `docs/source-map.md` as the single term matrix, master
resolution with the address frozen per invoice, line and tax extraction
reading what ERPNext posted, and both document types reaching XML the
official rules accept.

**Review.** This is the phase I would review hardest, for one reason: it is
the only part whose correctness depends on ERPNext behaviour rather than on
a published rule, and it has never run against a real client's chart of
accounts, tax templates or item data.

Four things were found here after the fact, all recorded. Version 16 keeps
per line tax in a table rather than the map the spec warns about, which
made the spec's own warning obsolete (D050). A document discount is already
inside the line amounts (D051). The emirate rather than the state field
carries the country subdivision. And ERPNext clears its per line tax table
mid-submit, so reading an invoice during submission found no tax on any
line, which only a real submit exposed.

The pattern is that every defect here came from an assumption about
ERPNext, and the only thing that caught them was running against a real
document. That will happen again on a real site, and the mappings are
explicitly incomplete until one is available.

## P04 Draft workflow

**Asked for:** working record upsert with revision checks, Fast and Full
preview, save and submit gates, the native quick entry extension, company
setup, the invoice notice and dialog, and the item bulk fix.

**Built:** one working record per draft and none at all for a company that
is switched off, one validation service answering both the form and the
server, the submit gate following company policy, one native headline
notice and one button on the invoice, the quick entry extension, and the
bulk item fix.

**Review.** The invariant that matters most holds: a compliance gap never
stops a draft being saved. In Live an incomplete invoice cannot submit; in
Preparation it submits and says what would have stopped it; in both cases
the draft saves.

Two things to look at. The quick entry extension takes whatever class is
installed and puts itself on top, which preserves another app's extension
but means the behaviour depends on load order; that is how Frappe works,
but it is fragile and untested against a second app doing the same thing.
And zero fields on Sales Invoice is a fixed decision the whole design rests
on, so it is now tested directly rather than assumed.

## P05 Submission ledger

**Asked for:** frozen revisions and private artifact manifests, approval
and an atomic outbox claim, cancellation and correction with immutable
field controls.

**Built:** the freeze inside the invoice's own transaction with the control
row locked first, artifacts written and read back before work can be picked
up, approval bound to the exact content hash, an atomic claim with a
fencing token, and a cancellation policy that distinguishes what has gone
from what has not.

**Review.** The order in the freeze is right and is the part I would defend
hardest: lock, validate, write bytes, verify bytes, only then make the work
sendable. A rollback leaves no submission and no sendable intent, which is
tested.

One real gap remains and it is recorded rather than fixed. A rollback
removes the file records but the bytes stay on disk with nothing pointing
at them. Cleaning those needs a sweep that can prove a file is
unreferenced, and I did not build it. It is a housekeeping problem rather
than a correctness one, but it is real.

The recovery review later found three ways this phase could produce a
duplicate legal invoice, all through read then write races in correction
and cancellation. They are fixed and they are the most serious defects
found in the whole build.

## P06 Transport and simulator

**Asked for:** audited transport and result contracts, a stateful HTTP
simulator with XML and JSON adapters, then events, retries, reconciliation
and crash recovery.

**Built:** the adapter registry and the audited transport, a simulator over
a real socket covering thirteen awkward cases, two test adapters proving
one canonical document can go out as XML to one provider and as JSON to
another, the sender, the retry rules, and reconciliation.

**Review.** The send order is not negotiable and is implemented as
specified: claim, write the attempt, commit, then make the request holding
no lock, then record the result under a fencing check. A run of mine died
mid flight during testing and left the work held rather than retried, which
is the design working.

Two honest limits. Everything has run against a simulator I also wrote,
which proves the orchestration and proves nothing about a real provider.
And nothing receives an event yet, so the whole inbound event path,
including signature verification, is shape only.

## P07 Operations and upgrade

**Asked for:** readiness and reconciliation reports, notifications, pause
and resume, private downloads and redaction, hostile input and cross
company tests, upgrade, restore and clone safeguards.

**Built:** both reports, a daily summary scoped per manager, evidence that
cannot be made public, replaced or deleted, a download action that works
out the company itself, and the deployment safeguard that stops a restored
copy of a site from sending.

**Review.** The clone safeguard prevents the expensive failure: a
production backup on a staging box sending live invoices to real customers.
Production sending lives in the site configuration file and the value is
the site name it was granted for.

I described why that works incorrectly, in three places, and only found out
by taking a real backup and reading it. A database only restore leaves the
configuration behind. A backup taken with the configuration does not: it
copies site_config.json verbatim, so the sending key travels in it, and so
do the encryption key and the database password. The guard is not the key
being absent. It is the site name inside the key, which refuses a restore
under any other name, and the machine fingerprint in the database, which
pauses a restore under the same name on a different machine until a person
confirms the move. Both were already there and both were checked; only my
explanation was wrong. It is corrected in the operations guide and recorded
as D065, with a test pinning the fact so it cannot drift back.

Two things follow that a reviewer should weigh. A configuration backup is
as sensitive as the passwords inside it, which the operations guide now
says and which nothing enforces. And the one case neither guard covers is a
restore under the same name on the same machine, which I judge correct
because that is the site rather than a copy of it.

This phase also produced the leak I am least pleased about. The reports
used the list call that does not check permissions, so a restricted user
could list another company's submissions and see counts and document
numbers. Opening one was refused; listing them was not. It is fixed and
tested, and the distinction is written into `AGENTS.md`.

Upgrade testing is genuinely not done, because there is no previous release
to upgrade from. That belongs to the first release.

## P08 Scenario coverage

**Asked for:** one packet per enabled advanced scenario, extending the
source map, fixtures, routing and correction rules together.

**Built:** a scenario table read out of the pinned rules rather than
written from scratch. Thirteen rules key off the eight character
transaction string and each one says what its scenario makes necessary.
Seven of eight are supported; margin scheme is refused rather than
approximated.

**Review.** The method is the strength here. Every requirement cites the
rule that demands it, and on a real invoice each local finding fired
alongside the official rule it names, with the same rule id. That is the
only evidence I have that the table matches the publication rather than my
reading of it, and it is checkable by anyone.

Reverse charge, advances and retention are not built at all. They need the
accounting settled first and that is not a coding question.

## P09 Pre-ASP candidate

**Asked for:** both framework lanes and the workload suite, an independent
financial, security and recovery review, and an installable preparation
release with documentation.

**Built:** the acceptance suites for the cases that need a database, the
workload measurement, three independent reviews, the capability statement
generated from the code, the capacity record, and the coverage matrix.

**Review.** This phase is where the cost of building fast came due, and the
number is the honest summary: three independent readers, told to assume the
code was broken, found twenty five defects that 44 of my own tests and a
green build had not.

Three would have put a duplicate legal invoice at a provider. One wrote the
decrypted provider secret and a customer's invoice into a log any System
Manager can read, on any provider timeout, from four call sites I wrote
myself while fixing a different logging problem. One meant every foreign
currency invoice was refused outright by the official rules, because the
fifth worked example in spec 5.3 had no implementation behind it.

The workload measurement found its own: master queries grew by four per
row, so a five hundred line invoice asked the database two thousand and
fifty questions. It is flat at forty nine now.

The second honest number is twenty two: deliberate breaks made to check the
tests bite. Five were not caught first time, and every one of those five
was a test that looked like it covered something and did not. Those five
are listed in `docs/acceptance.md` because the misses are the useful part.

The Frappe v15 lane is not verified, because there is no v15 bench on this
host.

## P12 Receiving, brought forward

**Asked for by the spec at P12, after P09.** Built before P09 at your
request, recorded as D059.

**Built:** documents collected from the provider's inbox with a cursor,
read, matched to a supplier by tax number or network address, deduplicated,
and enterable as a draft Purchase Invoice in two steps.

**Review.** The reasoning for bringing it forward holds: buying is half of
what this app is for on a UAE site, and finding out at P12 that the model
did not fit would have meant redoing work from P03 onward.

Nothing arriving becomes a purchase on its own. No supplier is created, no
item is invented, nothing posts. Six separate reasons can stop an entry and
each is reported on its own rather than as one wall.

One thing a reviewer should weigh rather than accept: a sender who knows
one of your company tax numbers chooses which company their invoice lands
against, and an exact item code match lets them choose the item. A person
still submits the draft, so I treat it as accepted risk rather than a hole,
but it should be a decision rather than something nobody noticed.

Self-billing is not built. It needs a standards package this release does
not carry.

## Departures from the specification

Four, all deliberate and all recorded.

**Receiving before P09** rather than at P12 (D059), at your request.

**The working rhythm from 18 September**: one pull request per sub phase
merged on a green lint and the existing tests, with the testing deferred to
P09. That is not what spec 1.1 describes. It is recorded in
`docs/build-state.md` so a later reader knows why the phases between P02
and P08 carry less evidence than the ones before.

**No review gate.** Spec 1.1 asks for a maintainer review and a second
competent review for tax, identity, recovery and permissions. You removed
the gate on 17 September. The three independent reviews at P09 are the
closest thing to what the spec asked for, and they arrived after the code
rather than before.

**A fils stated as a line adjustment** (D063), where ibr-147-ae cannot
otherwise be satisfied on a tax inclusive invoice. This changes what the
document says, within what the rule's own arithmetic allows, and is the one
place where the app adds something ERPNext did not post. The bound is
narrow and the wider case is still reported, but it deserves a second
opinion.

## What is not proven

- Anything on a real provider. Every send has been to a simulator.
- Anything on a real client's data. No real chart of accounts, tax
  template or item catalogue has been near this.
- Frappe v15.
- Capacity on the reference host in spec 11.2. The measurements in
  `docs/capacity.md` are from a laptop and say so.
- A site holding a hundred thousand invoices, concurrent sessions, or a
  multi company site under load.
- Upgrade from a previous release.
- Event integrity, because nothing receives an event.
- A real restore onto a second machine. It needs a second site, which needs
  the database root password, which I should not be handling. The guards
  are tested in place; the restore itself is not.
- That kept evidence stays on disk. The integrity checker found a demo
  submission whose reference XML is recorded with a checksum and whose
  bytes are gone. The freeze path reads every file back before accepting
  the freeze, so it was there when written. I cannot say what removed it.
  Demo data, but the File hooks guard the record and not the bytes under
  it, and that is worth a reviewer's judgement.

## My comments

**On the fast track.** It worked for speed and the bill was large. Twenty
five defects reached the P09 gate that would not have reached it under the
spec's own rhythm. None of them shipped, because P09 caught them, but that
depended on three independent readers being told to assume the worst. If
this pattern continues past P10, I would want that review step to be part
of the plan rather than a rescue at the end.

**On what I got wrong.** The worst defect in the build was mine and it came
from a fix. I removed raw error text from a user facing field and put the
whole request, including the decrypted client secret, into the error log
instead. I did not know Frappe renders every local variable when you call
its logger with only a title. That is the kind of thing a second reader
catches and I did not.

I then made the same mistake again, in the last hour, in a test. Frappe
prints every local in a traceback as well as in the error log, and a test
I wrote to prove a point about backups parsed site_config.json into a
local. It failed in CI for an unrelated reason and put that runner's
throwaway database password into the public run log. The value is a
disposable CI default and not a client credential, but the shape of the
error is exactly the one I had already been caught by once. It is fixed,
and the fix is checked by forcing the assertion to fail and reading the
output. A reviewer should take from this that I do not reliably remember
that Frappe prints locals, and that the rule belongs somewhere a tool can
enforce rather than in my head.

**On a red build I did not look at.** Site tests were already failing on
`develop` when I opened this document, and I did not know. The failure was
a fixture posting into a fiscal year the CI site does not have, which
arrived with the calendar rather than with any change. I merged the
previous branch on a green pure build without reading the site job. Two
things follow: the failure is fixed and the fixtures now build the year
they need, and my own standing rule about checking exit codes rather than
the tail of the output did not save me, because I did not look at the
second job at all.

**On the tests.** Six hundred and fifty five tests is not the claim. The
claim is twenty two deliberate breaks with five misses, all five now
covered. I would rather a reviewer spent time on the five misses in
`docs/acceptance.md` than on the count.

**On the spec.** Two small things in version 2.1. Section 9.4 cites a
section 4.5 that does not exist. And section 5.2 warns against a tax field
version 16 replaced with something better, so the warning is now misleading
rather than wrong.
